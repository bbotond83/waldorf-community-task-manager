What each tab of the community's spreadsheet does today, agreed with the owner one tab at a time.
Based on the anonymised structure report; contains no names or contact details.

## 1. Szülők elérhetősége (parent contacts)

**Contents:** one row per parent (about 30 parents, 15 children, so about 15 families). Columns: parent name, child
name, phone, email, place of residence (town or village), occupation, hobby, "Amivel a közösséget támogatod" (how I
support the community). Occupation, hobby and support are optional and filled in for some parents only. The two row
colours alternate by family, so the two parents of one family are easy to see together.

**Who uses it, and how:**
- The whole community uses it to see each other's contact details, children, occupation, interests, place of
  residence and what each parent can offer the community.
- Every user should be able to see it.
- The occupation, hobby and support fields are used both to find someone with a needed skill and to get to know
  each other.

**Who maintains it:**
- The administrator adds parents with the main details (name, email, family assignment).
- Parents fill in and update the rest of their own details, and can change their own email and password.
- It is not known today who adds or removes families; in the app the administrator should do it.

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**
- The parent area today has no directory page and no "my details" page.
- The app keeps occupation, hobby and support in a single free-text optional profile with consent.
- Showing every family's contact details to every parent needs a clear basis under GDPR (for example each parent's
  consent per field); the app currently asks for consent only for the optional profile.

## 2. Kinti - benti takarítás 2025-26 (indoor and outdoor cleaning during the school year)

**Contents:** one row per week (52 numbered weeks, each with a date range). One column per job: indoor cleaning,
front garden, back garden. Each cell names the family on duty that week, written today by a child's name. Beside the
rota: a list of the children and a list of the children leaving the kindergarten.

**How it works today:**
- The administrator creates the schedule for the whole school year.
- One family is solely responsible for one job in a given week; a different family does the other job that week. One
  family never has two jobs in the same week.
- There is no fixed day: the family makes sure the job is done some time during its week.
- From March to October the front-garden family also waters the front garden during its week (watering
  instructions in appendix A).
- A family that cannot do its week swaps with another family.
- Empty weeks are school holidays. Indoor cleaning is not needed during school breaks.
- The list of leaving children is there so that leaving families are not put on duty after 1 July.
- The rota involves every family, so it belongs to no working group.

**Related events (one-off, not weekly):** winter, spring and summer big cleaning (téli, tavaszi, nyári
nagytakarítás), and the shared garden day at the end of August (közös kertrendezés).

**Decisions and wishes for the app:**
- Assign duties to families by family name, not by a child's name.
- A leaving family cannot be assigned a duty after 1 July; a new family cannot be assigned one before 1 July.
- The back garden is out of scope for now: one family looks after it all year in return for a reduced fee for its two
  children.

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**
- Whether the app can hold a year-long weekly rota (one family per job per week, holidays left out) and how the
  administrator would create it.
- Whether the app knows when a family joins and leaves, which the 1 July rule needs; whether 1 July is fixed or set
  per school year is still to be decided.
- Whether the app's swap flow matches "swap with another family for a whole week".

## 3. Állatos hétvégi beosztás 2025-26 (weekend animal care)

**Contents:** one row per week with its date range. Columns: the family on weekend animal care; the family on indoor
cleaning that week ("Hétvégi óvoda takarításért felelős család"); the family on front garden that week ("Hétvégi kert
rendezésért felelős család"); for each of those two families, "melyik napot vállalja" (which duty they take); a
column marking school-break weeks; the children list.

**How it works today:**
- Animals need care every weekend, school breaks included.
- Weekend animal care means letting the animals out in the morning with fresh water and food, shutting them into
  their pens in the evening, and cleaning the pens.
- A weekend has five duties: two openings (Saturday and Sunday morning), two closings (Saturday and Sunday evening)
  and one cleaning of the animal shelters.
- The family on indoor cleaning and the family on front garden that week must each take one opening or closing. They
  choose which one, but taking one is mandatory.
- The weekend animal-care family does the rest: the shelter cleaning plus two openings/closings normally, or plus
  three in a school-break week when there is no indoor cleaning. The shelter cleaning is always theirs and is the
  most significant part of weekend animal care; the other families cannot pick it.
- The weekend animal-care rota is planned and handled like tab 2: the administrator creates the yearly schedule and
  families swap.

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**
- The rota links three duties in one week: the open/close duties depend on who has indoor cleaning and front garden
  that week, and the number left for the animal-care family depends on whether it is a school-break week.
- Families must pick one duty from the four opening/closing slots; the app needs a "pick one, mandatory" sign-up with a
  way to see who has not picked yet.
- School-break weeks matter to several rotas and are marked by hand in each tab today.
- The anonymiser replaced the school-break markers with person placeholders, because they look like capitalised
  names.

## 4. Vízhordás 2025-26 (carrying spring water)

**Contents:** one row per week with its date range and the family on duty. The spring is the Petőfi forrás in Bag.
School-break weeks are marked in the family column itself. A second family column is a leftover from copying the tab
and has no meaning.

**How it works today:**
- The kindergarten keeps 7 or 8 glass bottles of 5 litres for spring water.
- During its week, the family on duty watches how much water is left and, if it runs low, fills the bottles at the
  spring.
- At the end of its week the family fills all the bottles in any case, so the next week starts with full bottles.
- No water is needed during school breaks.
- Planned and handled like tab 2: yearly schedule by the administrator, families swap, and the 1 July rule applies.

**Wish for the app:** track the cleaning of the bottles, which should be regular and is not tracked at all today.

**Recommendation for bottle cleaning (general hygiene practice, not an official rule; the owner decides):**
- At every refill: empty the bottle completely (do not top up old water) and rinse it with fresh water.
- Every four weeks, and every two weeks in summer heat: clean thoroughly with hot water and a bottle brush, then rinse
  well; a food-safe cleaning agent may be used if it is rinsed out completely.
- At once, out of turn, if a bottle shows a film, a green tint or a smell.
- Keep filled bottles cool and out of direct light.
- In the app this could be an extra duty attached to the water week of the family on duty every fourth week, which
  that family marks as done.

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**
- The same kind of weekly rota as tab 2, with one job.
- A recurring "clean the bottles" duty with a done mark is new; whether the app can mark a duty as done is to be
  checked.
- Water quality testing of the spring is the subject of tab 17 (Vízvizsgálat).

## 5. ÁLLATOK (daily opening, closing and feeding of the animals)

**Contents:**
- Rows 1 to 31 hold the instructions (full text in the appendix): the morning routine, the afternoon/evening routine,
  watering from March to October, and the Saturday-morning cleaning of the rabbit and hen shelters.
- From row 36 the schedule, one row per day, with the columns Dátum (date), Nap (weekday), Reggel (morning) and
  Délután (afternoon), plus a column marking school breaks ("Szünet").

**What it covers:** only opening and closing the animals with the daily feeding. (Weekend shelter cleaning is the
weekend animal-care rota of tab 3.)

**How it works today:**
- One family per slot (morning or afternoon) per day. The slot is written today by a family name or a family
  member's first name.
- The animals need care every day, school breaks included. This tab plans weekdays; weekend rows only repeat what
  tab 3 holds.
- Families sign up for free slots, but every family must contribute more or less evenly.
- Families often take the slot that fits their drop-off or pick-up, but this is not tracked.
- When a slot is empty or someone forgets, somebody has to chase it up. Removing that chasing is one of the main
  reasons for building the app.
- Watering is not part of this tab (see the appendix for the watering instructions). From March to October the
  family on front-garden duty that week (tab 2) waters the front garden; the back garden is watered by the one family
  that looks after it all year (out of scope for now).

**Decisions and wishes for the app:**
- Write slots by family name only.
- Show the instructions in the app.
- Do not add drop-off or pick-up tracking.

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**
- A sign-up rota of two slots per day for every day, with a fairness view (how many slots each family has taken).
- Reminders and an "unfilled slot" alert, so nobody has to chase families by hand.
- Instructions shown next to the matching duty (morning, afternoon, watering season, Saturday cleaning).

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**

## 8. Nagytakarítás (big-cleaning checklist)

**Contents:**
- Notes at the top: a fire warning (rags used with the vinegar furniture cleaner or with wax can ignite by
  themselves, so they must be hung out to dry before they are thrown away), linen curtains can be washed at 60 °C,
  and the contact of a carpet-cleaning company (kept in the original only).
- A checklist with the columns Terület és feladatok (area and tasks), Ki? (who), Mikor? (when) and Kész? (done).
  Column A lists about 90 tasks under ten areas (full list in appendix C). Some tasks are marked "NYÁRON" (summer
  only).
- Who, when and done are empty today: the tracker exists but is not used.

**Owner's idea for the app:**
- The checklist becomes part of a big-cleaning event. Families tick the tasks they did, and the app records who and
  when. The same list can work as a sign-up ("I'll do this one").
- Administrators or working-group admins can edit the list, so it is not fixed.

**Agreed with the owner:** make the checklist a general feature of any event, built from an editable checklist
template, instead of a separate "big cleaning" event type.
- A big cleaning (winter, spring, summer) is an event created from the "Nagytakarítás" template.
- Each task can be claimed (sign-up) and ticked as done; the app records who and when.
- Areas become sections; "summer only" tasks are flagged and appear only in the summer event.
- An event takes a copy of the template when it is created, so later edits to the template do not change past
  events.
- The same mechanism serves the shared garden day (közös kertrendezés) and other events that need a task list.

**Notes for the gap analysis (to verify against the app):**
- Whether events can have sub-tasks, claims and done marks with who/when.
- Template editing rights for administrators and working-group admins.
- The safety and washing notes belong on the information/FAQ page.

## 9. Nagytakarítás szervezés (organising the big cleanings)

**Contents:** an information tab written by a previous organiser (full text in appendix D): when the big cleanings
happen (winter, spring and summer break), special points for particular tasks, and the organiser's to-do list.

**Agreed with the owner: split the content by who needs it and when.**
- **Organiser's checklist on the big-cleaning event:** send the notice at least two weeks before; set a sign-up
  deadline and check that every area is covered; write again if there are not enough sign-ups; make Saturday the last
  cleaning day (Sunday stays free for the teachers and for the organiser's closing round); clean together with the new
  parents in summer; collect the spiral before the winter cleaning; closing round once everything is marked done
  (check the kindergarten, finish or follow up what was missed, check that everything is back in its place).
- **Tips attached to the matching checklist tasks in the template:** canopy (bring it in damp, iron and hang it at
  once; it goes up from the top of the big ladder, never alone), wool rugs and sheepskins (washed by hand with
  lanolin), the rag rug in Fényes (mixed fabrics stretch differently when wet), toy fabrics (taken home and washed at
  every big cleaning), doll-furniture fabrics (wash in summer, dust during the year), carpet cleaning only in summer.
- **Information/FAQ page:** when the big cleanings happen; before the winter and summer festivals the furniture is
  moved into Alvós, so whoever cleans Alvós (or needs their furniture back) moves it out; the carpet-cleaning company
  comes to the kindergarten.

**What the app can take over from the organiser's list:**
- Send the notice and reminders itself, show the sign-up deadline, and alert the organiser about unclaimed tasks.
- Start each event with an empty checklist (a fresh copy of the template), so nobody has to clear the table.
- Show when everything is marked done, which is the cue for the closing round.
- Let each sign-up carry a date and time, because some tasks depend on others (furniture moved out of Alvós first,
  windows and curtains before the rest of a room).
- Let a family record how many rag rugs it took home to wash, which the old notes suggest keeping in a separate sheet.

**Out of scope:** renegotiating who pays for professional carpet cleaning (money is out of scope).

## 13. Nyárünnep 2026 (summer festival, Sunday 21 June 2026)

**Contents (details of the latest festival given by the owner, names left out):**
- **Games plan:** about ten stations, each with a responsible person, a place and props. Examples: treasure hunt in the
  sandpit (marbles, pebbles), getting through a spider web (bamboo, string), willow cave (tarpaulin to cover it),
  branch labyrinth (branches), carrying water from one place to another (buckets, cups, tub), golden flowers from the
  tree (paper flowers, fishing line), a framing story, music with an invited musician, fire jumping, packing up. Places
  include the sandpit, the hedge at the front, the willow tunnel, both sides of the pavilion and the climbing tree.
- **Food sign-up ("Finomságok"):** a notice that everyone brings their own cup and plate, and an allergen notice (milk;
  egg, unless baked long at high heat; garlic, although powder or granules are fine). Families write what they bring
  under eight categories: spreads, bread, free-from, savoury, sweet, drinks, vegetables/fruit, other. Many entries say
  whether they are vegan.
- **Flower gate:** made on Sunday morning, with the names of the people who make it.
- **Other things to bring:** 2-3 blue-dyed tablecloths, a 5-litre glass jar with a tap, an extra table.
- A column of about 27 lines that looks like a song or poem.

**How it fits the app (agreed with the owner):**
- An event built from a "summer festival" template: games become checklist tasks with a responsible person, a place
  and props; the flower gate is a task with a time and several makers; the items to bring are tasks someone can claim.
- The food sign-up is a sign-up with a category and a free-text note per entry, with the event's notices (bring a cup
  and plate, allergens) shown at the top.
- The festival-preparation working group owns it ("Búcsúzás/nyárünnep" in its document); its coordinator creates the
  event from last year's plan as a template.

**Answers from the owner:**
- Parents volunteer for a game station; one person per station.
- Food offers are visible to everyone. Each offer gets tick boxes for allergens (milk, egg, garlic) besides the free
  text. The eight categories are fixed; "other" covers the rest.
- The song/poem column is the year's tale: a storyteller acts as the main game master and leads the children through
  the games as if they were a story. The event needs a game-master assignment and a free-text field for the year's
  tale.
- Taking part is voluntary and is not counted anywhere.

**Notes for the gap analysis:**
- Responsible people can be outside the app (for example an invited musician), so a task may need a free-text name.
- Allergen information must stay general (which ingredients to avoid), never which child is allergic, because a
  child's health data needs special protection under GDPR.

## 14. Kertfejlesztés (the community's to-do list for repairs and improvements)

maintenance and the animals (full list in appendix G). Same columns as tab 11: topic, status, priority 1-3,
sub-task, tools and materials, plus who, when, cost, progress note and other note. About 50 tasks; most are done.

**Agreed with the owner: one shared to-do list in the app, separate from events.**

  the default view but stay in the history.
- Tasks can be pulled into a community work-day event (the playground group organises these), where they appear on
  the event's checklist.

- Owners by topic (confirmed): playground garden group for the ship surroundings, sandpit, teepee,
  willow tunnel, playhouse surroundings, flowers, street front, hedges, paving and mowing; maintenance group for
  structural repairs (pavilion, ship structure), mosquito nets, pen fence, water-tank tap, playhouse renovation,
  compost bin and moving the tool shed; animal-care group for hens and rabbits; raised beds, herbs and back-garden

**Answers from the owner:**
- Kitchen-garden and back-garden tasks stay on the list, owned by the back-garden family, which can claim them and
  update their status. So a task's owner can be a working group or one family. Kitchen-garden and back-garden work is
  also done occasionally by the community at the shared garden day (közös kertrendezés).
- A parent's own task list shows only the duties assigned to them and the tasks they claimed. The shared to-do list of
  open tasks is a separate page.
- When the community moves from the spreadsheet to the app, the full list is loaded, done tasks included, so the
  history is not lost.
